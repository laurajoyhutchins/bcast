# Source and Rights Policy

BCAST works with building codes, standards, laws, amendments, and related technical publications that may have different copyright, licensing, access, and redistribution rules.

This public repository must not become a distribution channel for material BCAST does not have the right to publish.

## Source authority

Publishers, standards organizations, governments, and other lawful providers remain authoritative for their source publications. BCAST does not grant rights to third-party source material and does not imply ownership of publisher content.

## Material that must not be contributed

Do not submit:

- licensed or restricted source text, tables, figures, page images, or bulk excerpts;
- content copied from paid or access-controlled provider APIs unless redistribution is explicitly permitted;
- provider credentials, API keys, tokens, entitlements, cookies, account identifiers, or account-specific locators;
- private provider responses or exports;
- production normalized datasets derived from restricted sources;
- production provider-to-BCAST mappings;
- private review corpora, reviewer annotations, or acceptance decisions;
- other material whose publication rights are unclear.

When rights are uncertain, do not publish the material here.

## Acceptable examples

Public examples should be one of:

- project-authored synthetic data;
- public-domain material;
- material with clearly documented redistribution permission;
- minimal source-safe metadata or provenance that cannot reconstruct protected content and does not reveal private access patterns.

A citation or source URL does not by itself establish redistribution rights.

## Provenance and identifiers

Hashes, public publication identifiers, edition metadata, and source-safe provenance may be published when useful for interoperability. They must not be used to smuggle protected content, private provider locators, or account-specific access information into the public repository.

Provider-native identifiers remain provenance or aliases. They do not become canonical BCAST identity merely because they are public.

## Project-authored licensing

The license eventually selected for BCAST's public artifacts applies only to material BCAST has the right to license. It does not sublicense third-party building-code, standards, publisher, or government content beyond the rights actually held.

No public project license is selected yet. The repository remains private until that decision is made and the publication checklist passes.
